The shape of the investment

The platform is built on managed services that are free at this scale, so the spend is almost entirely people, not infrastructure. Monthly running cost stays under ₹2,200 through every phase in this document, and the worst case is capped — the system can be moved onto a self-managed server for roughly ₹700 a month without rewriting anything.

Nothing here bills by usage. Costs are fixed monthly amounts, not per-request charges, so adding cities or staff does not increase the bill. This was a deliberate technical choice — several popular alternatives charge per database read, where a busy dashboard can produce an unpredictable invoice.

Assumptions and exclusions

Assumed
  • One pilot city, Bhopal, before wider rollout
  • Under 50 staff using the portal during Phases 0–1
  • Customer records hold contact and service details only
  • Managers enter customer data; customers do not log in until Phase 3
  • Rupee figures at ₹88 to the US dollar
Not included
  • Medical or clinical records — a separate compliance review
  • Billing, invoicing or payment collection
  • Payroll and attendance
  • Integration with hospital or insurer systems
  • Data migration from existing spreadsheets, if any
  • Ongoing support and maintenance after each phase
The main risk to these figures is scope, not technology. Phases 2 and 3 are estimated against a written plan, not against watching real managers work. The single most useful thing the Bhopal pilot produces is a corrected estimate for everything after it.
