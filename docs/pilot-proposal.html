How a release reaches your team

One screen at a time, not all at once

Every new screen ships behind a switch. It stays invisible to your staff until you decide otherwise — so a release date and a rollout date are two different things, and a rollout can be undone in minutes.

  1. Ship it, switched off The code is live but no screen changes for anyone. Nothing to retrain, nothing to explain.
  2. You look at it privately A private link shows the new screen to you alone, on the real system with real data.
  3. Switch it on when your team is ready One screen at a time, in an order that makes sense — records before attendance, attendance before billing.
  4. Switch it back off if it is not working No rebuild, no rollback, no data lost.

Before the pilot starts

Two things to settle

User logins

The portal records a manager or staff member, but issuing them a login is currently a manual step done by us. For a pilot with a handful of users that is workable. Before you onboard people at any scale, this should become self-service — an admin sends an invite and the person sets their own password. It is a small, separate piece of work and we would recommend doing it alongside the pilot rather than after it.

Test accounts

The system currently holds demonstration accounts and sample records so it can be explored safely. These must be removed and real accounts created with proper passwords before any genuine patient or staff information is entered.

Open questions for Phase 3. Before billing is built we need your answers on five operational points: whether a patient is charged when a staff member is absent and a replacement is sent; whether a 24-hour day is ever billed above its rate; how a bill reaches the family; whether payments are reconciled against a bank statement; and whether a rate change applies from a date or from the next month. GST is excluded from this proposal.
